# Infinite Gacha — Final Direction (Claude × Astra, 8 rounds, 2026-09-24)

**1. The pitch and the spine**

*Infinite Gacha is a living-world squad RPG where every summon brings someone worth knowing, every battle changes their relationships, and the town you build can rise to rescue them.*

**Spine:** Summon a person → give them a home → discover whom they love and what they want → fight together → let their choices, achievements and losses change the world.

**2. The six pillars**

**The Door Opens Onto Lives** — Every summon changes two worlds.

- Open a homeland rift; glimpse someone mid-adventure before rank, name, passion and dream appear.
- Summon extraordinary power—and the pursuer who knows how to defeat it.
- Watch pity become a beacon: a particular recruit approaches a guaranteed arrival.
- Pull distinct relatives, rivals and pupils instead of duplicate souls.
- Face an entire homeland demanding its missing person back.
- **Joy:** rift potlucks bring singing bread, impossible recipes and embarrassing reunions.

**A Home Worth Returning To** — Build streets full of people who have reasons to stay.

- Neighbors become friends, rivals and collaborators; adjoining workshops invent techniques.
- Fulfill a dream by founding a tavern, school or forge that seeds other people’s dreams.
- Feed households through winter; design evacuation routes around actual rescue promises.
- Watch apprentices found independent settlements that reinterpret your traditions.
- Call a retiree into one legendary battle: their pupils and institutions combine into **The Founder Walks Again**.
- **Joy:** soup factions, petty hobbies, monster pets, nickname traditions and festivals arise from residents’ histories.

**People Inside the Fight** — Their relationships change what your squad can do.

- Program skills across bonded heroes: one commits, another counters the retaliation.
- Pilot a frightened veteran through the killing pattern they finally recognize.
- Turn a dream into a rescue chain: break pursuit, lift a friend, cast while carrying.
- Let an apprentice answer the missing beat in a grieving survivor’s old combo.
- Confront a refusal with a visible reason: a hero breaks formation to save someone, and may be right—or tragically mistaken.
- **Joy:** squads invent sports and ridiculous skill exhibitions; apprentices parody a nemesis’s famous pose.

**Power Has Witnesses** — Everyone remembers how you became extraordinary.

- Raise an overlooked F-rank through deeds into a **Risen** SSS whom fate can no longer ignore.
- Earn Essence through living achievements, or sacrifice someone for irreversible power.
- Face protests, hidden victims, purposeful volunteers and desertions that found rival towns.
- Unleash the **Parliament of Ghosts**: sacrificed people perform their real techniques, cooperating or resisting according to their promises.
- Discover that you—the conscious Door—can be loved, hunted, rescued or overthrown by your own heroes.
- **Joy:** the terrifying champion still has a ridiculous nickname and a pet that steals their chair.

**Fate Has a Face** — Outwit a Guest who engineers collisions from your actual history.

- Choose Mother Briar’s tangled affections, the Gilded Jackal’s outrageous wagers or Sister Cinder’s unfinished promises.
- Read omens, bargain over opportunities and discover the causal chain behind an encounter.
- Hunt named, scarred nemeses who remember your tactics—and may eventually retire to teach counters to them.
- Recover a stolen name through the habits and techniques its forgotten owner left behind.
- Raise a Risen hero capable of stealing the Guest’s chair.
- **Joy:** the Guest can arrange a disastrous reunion banquet as readily as a siege; residents create the punchline.

**Nothing Ends Without Leaving Something** — Every life changes the world’s possibilities.

- Visit graves with friends; face hollow bosses carrying the dead hero’s real kit and gear.
- Welcome the Winter of Names, when unfinished dreams return to familiar homes.
- Discover a dead defender’s protection in their doorway; eventually pilot **The Town Gets Up** to rescue an expedition.
- Read Chronicle chapters grounded in deeds, with witnesses who disagree about their meaning.
- If approved, enter **The Old World**: former towns become homeland rifts, your champion becomes a future nemesis, and a chair-thief becomes the next town’s Guest.
- **Joy:** recipes, jokes and festivals outlive their founders. One soul remains one person throughout the afterlife.

**3. The addiction stack**

- **30 seconds:** reveal a person through a rift; recognize a telegraph; land a personal combo.
- **5 minutes:** check a household, discover a dream complication, select the next squad or summon.
- **Session:** pursue a promise, return with changed relationships, build or celebrate the consequence.
- **“At work”:** “Her brother arrives tomorrow. Will she still volunteer?” Anticipation persists without closed-game punishment.
- **Hour 5:** squad sports reveal personality after basic automation becomes familiar.
- **Hour 20:** former apprentices build an independent town you want to visit.
- **Hour 100:** a retired nemesis’s school teaches your enemies—and your pupils.
- **Hour 500:** a congress of homeland societies, or former worlds if approved, contests the Door’s future.

**4. The first hour**

- **Minute 0:** summon someone with a passion, dream and homeland connection.
- **Minute 5:** place their home and workplace; witness a specific interaction with another resident.
- **Minute 15:** deploy, read a telegraph, pilot a hero and program a short learned-skill chain.
- **Minute 30:** choose how to tackle an exposed rescue or dangerous objective. Actual play produces protection, near-loss or loss; no death is scripted.
- **Minute 60:** return to a relationship change, dream milestone and Chronicle entry. An omen connects tomorrow’s expedition to what actually happened.

**5. The build order**

Starting from today’s summons, permadeath, sacrifice, real-time expeditions, newly landed houses/lumbermill/wood, and the accepted skill system under construction:

1. **The Ledger.** Record settled events—who did what to whom, when and where—including summons, downings, rescues, sacrifices and shared meals as those systems arrive. History readers derive their accounts from those facts; combat still resolves combat. Surface a simple personal history. **New feeling:** “The game remembers what we did.”
2. **People at home.** Add passions, quirks, dreams, meals, neighborhood encounters and Bonds around the town foundations. **New feeling:** affection and everyday amusement.
3. **People in combat.** Complete the skill foundation; add personal chains, informed refusals, rescue motives and recognition. **New feeling:** trust in a particular squad.
4. **Summoning with consequences.** Add homeland populations, anchors, beacon pity, entourage arrivals and pursuers. Connect deeds and living achievements to promotion. **New feeling:** anticipation about who arrives next.
5. **Ambitions become institutions.** Add Founding, apprentices, collaborative schools, food pressure, seasons, raids and independent settlements. **New feeling:** pride in a society with its own future.
6. **Death and power remain personal.** Add graves, sacrifice reactions, Parliament, hollows, Winter visitors and inherited techniques. **New feeling:** grief, responsibility and discovery.
7. **Fate becomes an opponent.** Add the selectable Guests, causal schemes, evolving nemeses, stolen names, Door overthrow and the chair challenge. **New feeling:** rivalry with a world that knows you.
8. **History becomes spectacle.** Realize the Founder’s formation, walking town and homeland congress; extend into Old World succession if approved. **New feeling:** astonishment at what these lives built.

**6. Owner decisions still open**

- **Old World succession:** layered campaigns and personal rediscovery versus keeping direct ownership of one roster forever.
- **Living Essence and deed-based promotion:** flourishing creates power alongside sacrifice versus the current sacrifice-centered progression economy; formulas remain undecided.
- **Hero refusal and Door overthrow:** people can permanently redirect player plans versus reliable command authority.
- **One continuous afterlife:** irreversible living death with meaningful supernatural agency versus a stricter final separation; define whether a departed soul can ever become recruitable again.
- **Risen-only chair theft:** preserves the underdog culmination but excludes heroes born above F from that specific destiny.

**7. Cut list**

- **Alternate selves:** undermine irreplaceability; replace with worlds transformed by one person’s absence.
- **Anonymous duplicate fodder:** contradicts personhood; replace with distinct entourage members.
- **Permanent F-rank invisibility:** contradicts remembered deeds; replace with recognition earned through audacity.
- **Universal ascension or compulsory death after retirement’s final fight:** treats fulfillment as disposal; replace with Founding and the living legendary formation.
- **Generic banners and seasons disconnected from people:** merge into homeland arrivals and the shared winter of hunger, visitors and promises.
- **Separate joy pillar:** would isolate happiness from ordinary life; weave generated joy through all six.
