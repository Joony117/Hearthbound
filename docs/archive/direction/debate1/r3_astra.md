**Your retiree is still being rewarded with a better death.** Dream-seeding works. “Soft target” plus “final chapter” makes happiness a fuse. Keep the legendary return, but let victory found a living legend: the retiree changes what the town can do forever. Their last *battle* can begin decades of other people’s stories.

**A — The Storyteller: The Guest at the Empty Chair**

A supernatural visitor lives in your tavern and wagers with fate. You choose which Guest occupies the chair. Heroes can notice, resent, court and eventually challenge it.

- **Reads:** actual histories and relationships: who rescued whom, witnessed a sacrifice, shares a homeland, inherited a technique, abandoned a promise, or has an empty place at dinner. Also reads current opportunities: an exposed rift, an approaching winter, a nemesis seeking recruits.
- **Pulls:** whom a rift connects to, which existing enemy pursues a lead, who receives an invitation, what an expedition uncovers, when a visitor arrives, and which competing ambitions gain an opportunity. It creates situations; heroes respond through their own motives.
- **Generation rule:** every collision needs a causal chain. A lost sibling exists in the homeland’s population before their identity is revealed. The Guest can bring them together; it cannot retroactively declare an unrelated killer their brother.
- **Escalation:** remember how you resolved previous collisions. If you keep rescuing enemies, the Guest assembles a banquet of people who owe you their lives—and gives them incompatible requests.

**Three Guests**

- **Mother Briar, the Matchmaker:** believes love becomes magnificent under pressure. **Signature:** places two bonded heroes’ dreams on opposite sides of the same siege; their choices can produce a third, shared ambition. **Do/feel:** build an alliance neither could achieve alone; feel anguish become invention. **Loop:** check what their relationship now makes possible. **Feeds:** Bonds, Dreams, raids, rival towns.

- **The Gilded Jackal, the Wagerer:** believes every miracle deserves an outrageous audience. **Signature:** your nemesis offers a truce to help recover the person you both love, then stakes their crown on who brings them home. **Do/feel:** deploy beside someone you hate; feel greed, suspicion and competitive exhilaration. **Loop:** one more expedition to settle the wager. **Feeds:** Nemesis, Rifts, rescue, Chronicle.

- **Sister Cinder, the Keeper:** believes nothing loved should vanish without changing the world. **Signature:** winter brings a sacrificed hero to the house of someone their champion saved. **Do/feel:** arrange their meeting and pursue the new promise it creates; feel an old decision acquire an unexpected meaning. **Loop:** visit the next household touched by that legacy. **Feeds:** Parliament, graves, Winter, apprentices.

**Let players feel the hand:** the Guest lays an illustrated omen on its table before a collision; afterward, the Chronicle exposes the causal chain. Players can bargain away something specific to redirect its attention. Outcomes remain consequences of play. Eventually, heroes can dream of stealing the Guest’s chair. **Loop:** learn its tastes, outwit its next wager, raise someone capable of becoming tomorrow’s storyteller.

**B — Thirty seconds at the rift**

- **0–5 seconds: choose whose world to reach.** Your heroes gather around the portal. Select a homeland connection and see its known dangers and rank chances. A resident brings a personal anchor: a recipe, broken insignia, unfinished letter.
- **5–12: glimpse a life in motion.** Beyond the tear, someone is doing something generated from their occupation, dream and immediate situation—holding a bridge, stealing a relic, feeding a monster. Silhouette and power effects build anticipation.
- **12–20: the arrival.** Rank erupts across the screen; name, passion and dream follow. Then one resident reacts: recognition, fascination, contempt—or simply an offered hand. An F-rank can land harder than an SSS because someone whispers, “You kept it.”
- **20–30: the second reveal.** A thread remains through the portal: their pursuer, promised companion, stolen possession or unfinished rescue. Reveal a concrete lead; conceal the unresolved identity or consequence, with a discoverable route beyond more pulls.
- **Pull-again loop:** another summon can reach someone connected to that thread. Expeditions can investigate it too. Each pull expands an actual population and its relationships; the excitement is discovering who enters *this* town.

**C — Cut and merge**

- **Retire “ascension” as universal completion.** Replace it with **Founding**: fulfilled dreams create institutions that generate other people’s ambitions. The wild payoff is a town whose culture your heroes authored.
- **Merge graves, Winter, Parliament and the living town into one afterlife.** A dead person has one continuing identity, expressed through different encounters. A hollow containing a soul must be confronted before that soul can inhabit its old doorstep. Their whereabouts become an expedition mystery.
- **Merge apprentices and inherited dreams into living traditions.** Pupils can fulfill, reject or transform the founder’s purpose; rival towns can claim the same lineage.
- **Fold alternate selves and stolen names into Guest schemes**, using established world identities and explicit supernatural causes. They become attacks on relationships already built, with consequences that persist.
