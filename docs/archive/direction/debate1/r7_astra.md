**Joy belongs throughout all six pillars.** A seventh pillar would give us permission to confine happiness to festivals between tragedies. Heroes should amuse each other while working, summoning, practicing and remembering.

**A — Joy as a system**

Keep all five: soup factions, adopted monsters, ridiculous exhibitions, earned nicknames and frog lumber. Generate them from **preferences + relationships + available objects + remembered incidents**. Let heroes initiate, imitate, exaggerate and revive jokes. The Guest can arrange an opportunity; residents supply the comedy. Joy needs consequences that produce more joy: a ridiculous nickname becomes a team name, then a festival tradition.

- **The Enemy’s Terrible Fan Club.** Apprentices imitate a famous nemesis’s signature pose. A retired hero who actually fought them corrects everyone’s technique; the resulting exhibition is accidentally magnificent. **Do/feel:** arrange the performers and program increasingly absurd chains; enjoy seeing terrifying history become household nonsense. **Loop:** return after the next expedition to see what the children of that tradition parody next. **Feeds:** skills, Nemesis, apprentices, Chronicle.

- **The Monster Chooses the Wrong Master.** An adopted creature becomes infatuated with the town’s grumpiest resident, copying their patrol, stealing their chair and growling at their pet peeves. **Do/feel:** place its bed and watch the resident’s elaborate attempts to deny affection. Eventually they craft it a miniature uniform. **Loop:** check what the pair are doing today. **Feeds:** pets, passions, homes, Bonds.

- **The Rift Potluck.** Residents invite people from their homelands to bring a favorite dish. Ingredients interact with ordinary magic: floating dumplings, singing bread, soup that changes everyone’s voice. **Do/feel:** grow ingredients, choose hosts and arrange tables; laugh when your solemn champion squeaks through a ceremonial toast. **Loop:** one more homeland connection brings another culinary disaster. **Feeds:** Rifts, food, institutions, festivals.

**B — Where the game could go stale**

- **Hour 5: competent automation makes heroes interchangeable.** Escalate into **The Squad Invents Its Own Sport**. Existing skills, rivalries and terrain generate a local contest: shield surfing, aerial relay, monster herding. **Do/feel:** program exhibition chains and pilot the anchor contestant; discover personalities through playful mastery. **Loop:** beat the neighboring team’s invention. **Feeds:** combat, joy, Bonds, town layout.

- **Hour 20: successful town management becomes maintenance.** Let a living tradition found an independent settlement with its own interpretation of your methods. **Do/feel:** help former apprentices establish it, compete at festivals, share rescue routes—and negotiate when their ambitions conflict with yours. Pride becomes fascination with people beyond your command. **Loop:** visit what they built since your last expedition. **Feeds:** Founding, rival towns, Dreams, schools. Independence can grow from happiness as well as desertion.

- **Hour 100: even personal nemeses become familiar encounters.** **Your Nemesis Retires.** Their successor takes over the fortress; the old enemy opens an institution teaching counters to *your* famous techniques. **Do/feel:** send a pupil to learn, challenge the successor, or seek the veteran’s help against their own legacy. **Loop:** master the school founded around defeating you. **Feeds:** Nemesis, apprentices, retirement, combat.

- **Hour 500: history becomes a museum of completed stories.** With owner-approved Old World, stage **The Congress of Former Worlds**: successor settlements, surviving champions and traditions dispute stewardship of the Door. **Do/feel:** travel between worlds to assemble an alliance; fight beside former enemies against someone you once raised. Feel centuries become present company. **Loop:** another expedition changes who will stand with you. **Feeds:** all six pillars. Without prestige approval, use the same congress among the current campaign’s homeland worlds and independent settlements.

**C — My three biggest softenings**

- **I removed compulsory death after the retiree’s last stand without immediately matching its spectacle.** Restore **The Founder Walks Again**: their living pupils, institutional techniques and fulfilled promises combine into a single battlefield formation under their command. You deploy an entire tradition as their ultimate. **Feel/loop:** astonishment; cultivate another generation to discover what this person’s life can accomplish. No duplicated souls, and survival can still open a new chapter.

- **I demoted alternate selves and stolen names into occasional Guest schemes.** Alternate selves remain cut because they fight irreplaceability. Escalate their replacement into **The World Demands Its Missing Person Back**: the homeland crosses the rift with rival claimants, institutions and dependents. **Do/feel:** command a reunion that can become a siege, liberation or mass migration. **Loop:** pursue each claimant’s evidence. **Feeds:** Rifts, Dreams, defiance, town building.

- **I answered the living-town vision with a doorway and left the culmination vague.** Restore **The Town Gets Up**: the one resident soul animating it carries inhabited neighborhoods into battle. Houses become shield formations; working districts execute skill chains while residents operate them. **Do/feel:** pilot your town to rescue a stranded expedition. **Loop:** rebuild its formations through the relationships and schools inside it. **Feeds:** raids, jobs, afterlife, combat.

**Pitch:** *Infinite Gacha is a living-world squad RPG where every summon brings someone worth knowing, every battle changes their relationships, and the town you build can rise to rescue them.* You keep playing because the next pull introduces a person, the next fight reveals who they are, and returning home shows what everyone made of it—including something gloriously stupid you never ordered.
