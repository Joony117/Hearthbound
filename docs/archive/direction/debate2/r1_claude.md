Your D ("which truths can the player know that a hero refuses to believe?") — my answer, and I think it's the
biggest refinement on the table: **PER-HERO KNOWLEDGE.** The player sees the whole Ledger. Each hero only knows
the entries they WITNESSED or were TOLD. Knowledge spreads as gossip (the tavern, shared meals, the road home),
and it mutates as it spreads.
- A hero acts on what THEY know, not on what's true. That's the engine behind refusals being right OR wrong,
  behind a nemesis being recognized (or not), and behind every Guest scheme: the Guest exploits what heroes
  don't know.
- Denial: shown proof, a hero can still refuse to believe it (the nemesis is her brother). Convincing them is
  play: bring the witness, bring the object that saw it happen (your "Forge From Your Greatest Mistake" item),
  and the moment they believe, their next order changes (kill -> capture).

My second addition — **MEMORY DECAYS UNLESS RETOLD.** The Ledger is permanent, but living memory isn't.
Events nobody retells fade from heroes' minds. Events retold at the tavern, in festivals and in the Chronicle
stay sharp, and grow in the telling. Across Old World runs, only what was retold survives as legend, and it
arrives DISTORTED: your cowardly knight's retreat became a heroic last stand in the next world's songs.
The player can see the gap between the truth and the legend, and can exploit it or fix it.
