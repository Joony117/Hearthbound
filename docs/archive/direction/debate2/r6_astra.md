**A — The defector can belong to two sides until a battle makes that impossible**

**Trigger:** their new allegiance demands an action against an old bond or promise. **Rule:** their commitments remain intact after departure; training changes their kit and preferences through actual learning. Seeing a former bondmate downed can provoke a demand for ceasefire or prisoner exchange, followed by refusal if their commander rejects it.

**Screen:** they begin the chain you programmed, recognize its intended victim, and redirect toward the fallen person.

**Decide:** honor the proposed exchange, exploit it, or fight. Returning to your roster requires their agreement; they may instead found a school between the two settlements.

**Feel:** betrayal complicated by recognition. **Loop:** meet the person they are becoming. **Links:** Concordance, knowledge, schools, defiance.

All four living exits preserve the same person, possessions and relationships. Changed allegiance remains possible; reconciliation is something they choose.

**B — One soul, one current presence**

Living roster exits leave the soul **Alive**. Final death enters the following machine; sacrifice immediately binds the newly dead soul to its recipient.

| State | Entry, player action and exit |
|---|---|
| **Unmoored** | Death or a broken binding leaves the soul at its actual remains or released anchor. Recover that anchor, arrange burial or confront whoever captures it. Rites establish its next residence. |
| **Resting** | Burial provides a residence. Visit, hear testimony and address unfinished business. An appropriate vow can establish a new binding; a seasonal invitation can permit a visit. |
| **Parliament** | The soul inhabits a particular champion’s vessel. Negotiate its participation and promises. Relinquishing the binding permits transfer or release. Sacrifice’s Essence is awarded once; moving the soul never repeats it. |
| **Guardian** | A protective vow binds the soul to a particular place. Restore that place and train its living custodians. Fulfillment, relinquishment or a severed binding permits departure. |
| **Hollow** | A hostile rite confines the soul in an animated remnant. Fight or dismantle the binding. Breaking it returns the soul to Unmoored; it never returns to Alive. |
| **Winter visitor** | An eligible soul temporarily leaves its residence for a particular household. Hear it and pursue its unfinished purpose. It returns when the visit ends, or enters an agreed new residence. |
| **In transit** | The soul occupies one carried anchor while changing vessels or worlds. Escort, recover or intercept it. Its previous active role has ended; arrival establishes the next one. |
| **Released** | The soul accepts final release after its binding permits it. Active presence ends permanently. Commemorate its life; its recorded deeds and living traditions remain. |

**These are not additional residences:**

- A **grave** remains a memorial when its soul resides elsewhere.
- **Dream testimony** travels as communication from its source. Receiving it does not create another soul.
- **Stolen name** is a recognition condition that can affect any state without changing identity.
- **Old World succession** relocates only souls actually carried across; other souls remain where they are.
- A visitor temporarily vacates its active role. Its champion loses that contribution; its doorway loses the soul’s intervention, while learned living techniques remain.

If a host dies, resolve every bound soul individually: a preserved vessel can retain bindings, a destroyed one leaves souls Unmoored. If a visitor’s return vessel disappears, they become Unmoored at the visit’s end.

**Do/feel:** follow a particular person through these changes and negotiate their future. **Loop:** complete another obligation or recover another captive soul. **Links:** graves, Parliament, Winter, hollows, Old World.

**C — The hollow runs your preferences**

**Creation:** an enemy rite requires the actual soul, an available remnant and an unresolved purpose it can imprison. A raider can even sever a guardian binding and carry away the town’s protector. Every step is a recorded event.

The hollow uses the dead hero’s learned skills, bar modes and programmed chain preferences. It still uses the ordinary picker, telegraph answers, cooldowns and ability lock. Gear physically recovered elsewhere remains recovered; the hollow wears what its vessel actually holds.

**The fight:** its missing partner leaves a recognizable fallback when the old combo condition fails. A living friend who remembers the opening can perform the relevant learned technique, provoking the familiar follow-up and exposing the binding to an ordinary interrupt. Knowledge creates the opportunity; positioning and execution still win it.

**Screen:** your former hero begins a sequence you remember programming. The surviving friend moves into the empty position—and the familiar formation briefly appears across enemy lines.

**Decide:** exploit your knowledge brutally, bring someone capable of reaching the trapped person, or recover the anchor through the encounter’s available objectives.

Breaking the shell releases the soul from confinement and drops its held possessions. Arrange rest, address its vow or negotiate a new binding. Final release remains possible; recruitment never does.

**Feel:** recognition becoming confrontation, then responsibility. **Loop:** bring home the person’s unfinished consequence. **Links:** skills, Bonds, grief, storied places.

**D — Winter brings visitors with destinations**

**Trigger:** the live calendar enters winter, and a remembered invitation or unfinished promise connects an eligible soul to a household.

**Rule:** the soul must be able to leave its current binding. A trapped hollow cannot simultaneously visit; frost tracing toward its prison can reveal why the expected chair stays empty. The visitor occupies that household until the visit concludes or winter ends.

**Screen:** a familiar figure stands beside furniture that has changed owners. Through different piloted residents, the visit means reunion, accusation or an inexplicable empty place at dinner.

**Decide:** host the encounter, bring a disputed witness, show what became of an apprentice, or undertake the unfinished promise while the visitor can respond.

**What remains:** new testimony, changed beliefs, a taught custom, an altered dream or a fulfilled obligation permitting release. Failure leaves a particular unfinished relationship, not a generic seasonal penalty.

**Feel:** warmth, discomfort and limited time together. **Loop:** finish one more part of the promise before farewell. **Links:** homes, seasons, knowledge, Dreams. Closing the game freezes the entire visit.

**E — The Old World preserves people beyond your ownership**

A chapter ends when the Door departs and establishes its next anchor:

- Town fall or an overthrow can force departure.
- A fulfilled arc or successful chair theft can create a reason to leave.
- The player can choose departure with unfinished business still outstanding.

The old world persists. Buildings and stockpiles stay unless physically transported; people migrate only through actual departures. Canonical identities, deaths, deeds, relationships, beliefs, memory, institutions and outstanding covenants continue. The pity traveler’s promised approach also continues.

Your former champion can return **alive**, acting on their own ambitions, with precisely the souls still bound to their vessel. If dead, they can return only through a valid hollow binding. Released or relocated souls never reappear inside an obsolete snapshot.

**The 500-hour shape is a succession of relationships:**

- **First world:** one of the three original Guests teaches you how fate behaves.
- **First stolen chair:** someone you raised becomes a Guest whose preferred encounters express their actual life.
- **Later worlds:** additional Risen establish offices in worlds you helped shape. Former friends can become rival patrons; a teacher and pupil can arrange opposing opportunities.
- **By hour 500:** the important chairs are occupied by people whose childhood ambitions, battle habits and betrayals you remember. Their jurisdictions meet where worlds connect. They must use messengers, routes, inhabitants and available opportunities to interfere across those connections.

**Screen:** rival omens unfold across a congress table; each Guest uses the gesture or phrase they once used in your squad.

**Decide:** ally with a former companion, contest their covenant, reconcile feuding patrons or support another Risen’s claim. Your history supplies both leverage and reasons they refuse.

**Feel:** facing people who have grown beyond you. **Loop:** enter another world to answer something an earlier life set in motion. **Links:** every pillar. All world progression occurs during live simulation; no elapsed offline history is invented.
