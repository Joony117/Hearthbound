**B — Knowledge becomes terrain**

**Gossip travels with people.** A conversation requires contact: shared meals, work, travel, bedside visits. Each exchange carries a bounded number of topics, chosen by personal relevance, emotional intensity and the listener’s interests. A courier reaches another district faster than ordinary gossip; a popular tavern reaches more listeners. The screen shows **who knows which claim and through whom**.

**Mutation follows motives.** An eyewitness might omit their shame; a boastful teller exaggerates agency; a partisan substitutes a suspected culprit. Detail fidelity falls with repeated transmission, while confidence can rise through social reinforcement. Ten people repeating one source still provide one source’s evidence.

**Memory has layers.** Precise wording and sequence fade before gist; emotional associations and habits can survive after their explanation disappears. Varied retellings and meaningful reenactments strengthen recall more than repeating the same sentence. Forgetting a betrayal does not automatically restore trust. The permanent Ledger retains the fact; the next world inherits whichever legends people actually carried.

**Belief is played through encounters.** A hero displays their disputed claim, trusted sources and objection: “That cannot be her technique.” Bring the witness, the item or the person capable of demonstrating it. Independent corroboration changes confidence; identity-threatening evidence can cause denial. The hero must expose a concrete objection the player can test. Changed belief alters their next action—execution becomes capture, refusal becomes cooperation.

**Do/feel:** route testimony, choose audiences and stage demonstrations; feel responsible for what your town understands. **Loop:** check whom the story reached and what they did with it. **Links:** Guest, Chronicle, defiance, schools.

**Three moments only unequal knowledge creates**

- **The counter only one person understands.** A veteran recognizes the nemesis’s feint; their partner knows the heroic song’s incorrect version. **Do:** get the veteran’s signal through before committing the chain. **Feel:** urgent dependence on communication. **Loop:** teach the squad the real pattern. **Links:** telegraphs, legends, Bonds.
- **The volunteer hears the whole promise.** One hero offers sacrifice believing the champion will rescue a captive; the champion privately intends revenge. **Do:** arrange their meeting or conceal the conflict. **Feel:** complicity when consent changes. **Loop:** pursue the renegotiated promise. **Links:** Parliament, power, gossip.
- **The goat gets the medal.** A rescue’s retellings credit the knight; the saved apprentice knows a pet monster dragged them out. **Do:** stage the festival reenactment with the actual rescuer. **Feel:** affectionate embarrassment. **Loop:** watch the corrected joke become tradition. **Links:** pets, joy, Chronicle.

**C — Earn the walking town; trade consequential worlds**

**The Town Gets Up has a physical build path:**

1. Restore the actual home of a departed defender whose protection left a guardian legacy; its threshold becomes the **heart hearth**.
2. Found a school there. Living pupils learn and successfully perform the defender’s protective technique.
3. Build connected streets from that hearth to occupied districts. Place a crew station at each junction; assign trained residents.
4. Construct articulated supports beneath district plazas, then assemble feet at the settlement’s perimeter. Workshops make joints and braces; kitchens provision the crews.
5. Rehearse district movement, protection and attack as one programmed chain. Each station must successfully hand its action to the next.
6. Initiate the march. Streets fold, plazas rise, inhabitants scramble to their posts. Damaged districts lose their corresponding actions.

**Do/feel:** build, staff and command a community carrying its own home into a rescue. **Loop:** establish another district tradition to change its fighting form. **Links:** jobs, Founding, skills, afterlife. Living crews are playable; the dead guardian never is.

**Old World files carry** geography, institutions, item histories, identity records, skills, relationships, death states, the Guest and both factual and legendary accounts.

The visiting world can send traders, claimants and armies; contest your promises; teach its traditions; or march its own town through the rift. Its dead SSS can confront you as a hollow.
