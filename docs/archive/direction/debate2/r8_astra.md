**A — The move reveals a commitment you misunderstood**

**Trigger:** the hero perceives their deepest commitment under threat. **Rule:** the technique emerges at their next legal action, respecting cooldowns and the ability lock. It can override a programmed preference through the existing commitment rules; it never becomes an extra action outside combat timing.

**Escalation:** it may protect the person you ordered them to attack. The private lesson came from that enemy—or from someone the enemy also loved.

**Screen:** a familiar nervous gesture becomes the opening of an extraordinary technique. The hero says whom it is for.

**Decide:** respond to their choice, then approach them about teaching it. Agreement teaches particular people; witnessing it alone grants no command authority.

**Feel:** surprise followed by a changed understanding of someone familiar. **Loop:** discover why they kept it private and whom they will trust with it. **Links:** commitments, knowledge, mentors, defection.

**B — Five screens, five decisions**

Budgets count **simultaneously emphasized cues**, not visible people, abilities or physical hazards. All actual battle telegraphs remain visible.

| Screen | Always visible | Appears when relevant | Decision and hard budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Town** | Inhabited map, resource flows, live calendar, expedition status | A petition, changed household, proposed walking routes, approaching threat | **Where do I intervene?** Three emphases: selected person/place, urgent need, one changed scene. |
| **Battle** | Positions, health, actual telegraphs, objective, selected hero’s usable skill bar | A bond commitment, recognition, refusal or private technique affecting the current action | **What command matters now?** Three emphases: current lens, immediate threat, chosen action. |
| **Door** | Stones, 100-pull cost, ordinary rank chances, identifiable pity traveler when established | The chorus currently influencing arrivals, the person beyond the rift, a following pursuer | **Whose connection do I pursue?** Two emphases: the current arrival and the next unresolved connection. |
| **Hero** | Person, home, current work, expressed dream, known skills | A changed intention, specific relationship affecting a plan, an offered lesson | **How do I support this person?** Three emphases: their current concern, your proposed intervention, its immediate consequence. |
| **Chronicle** | Current chapter and actual event sequence | Conflicting testimony, an unfinished consequence, a connection to another chapter | **Which past event should guide our next action?** Two emphases: one event and one selected consequence. |

Town offers familiarity; battle offers informed commitment; the Door offers anticipation; the hero page offers understanding; the Chronicle offers recognition. Each leads directly back to a person, place or expedition where its decision can be acted upon.

**C — Reveal through people, not feature announcements**

These are pacing targets reached through eligible events, not timers that manufacture deaths or withhold already-earned mechanics.

- **Minute 0 — Ledger.** The first summon receives a name and a first recorded arrival. Clicking their story returns to the person standing at the Door.
- **Minute 5 — People at home.** Place their house; follow their route; notice whom they stop to speak with. Their first request explains why placement matters.
- **Minute 15 — People in combat.** Pilot them through a telegraph. Their partner’s response demonstrates a useful connection; programming the repeat explains chains through something just witnessed.
- **Minute 30 — Summoning with consequences.** A resident’s conversation influences an arrival. Recognition—or an approaching pursuer—connects the pull to an existing life.
- **Minute 60 — Institutions begin.** A fulfilled early ambition establishes a named practice or gathering place. Another resident asks to learn it. One person’s achievement has begun changing someone else.
- **Hour 5 — Death and power.** A meaningful loss, or an established homeland’s departed person, introduces the afterlife. A particular partnership makes the sacrifice choice understandable before its multiplier matters.
- **Hour 20 — Fate.** A Guest brings two familiar dreams into collision. The omen is readable because the player already understands both people. Counter-play introduces the Guest’s authority and obligations.
- **Hour 100 — Spectacle and succession.** A town rises, a former champion returns, or a Risen contests the chair. Each extraordinary event uses places, techniques and relationships the player helped establish.
- **Hour 500 — History becomes company.** Former heroes occupy the important chairs. Familiar gestures explain their schemes; their old relationships explain opportunities to oppose or reconcile them.

The build order supplies these capabilities. The player learns them through an incident, an action and its consequence.

**D — Notifications have three forms**

**“Act here.”** An urgent event produces one located cue: a portrait, short spoken reason and a destination or threatened action. Selecting it focuses the relevant place. Changes to the same incident update its cue instead of generating duplicates. Other urgent incidents retain visible markers even while one holds emphasis.

**“See what changed.”** A diary entry, new nickname, private meeting or household discovery waits. Returning to town highlights one actual changed scene: another chair at dinner, a mural, someone practicing outside the wrong school. Other events remain accessible through their people and Chronicle entries.

**“Something is approaching.”** An invitation attaches to a real person and live-time condition: “Her brother reaches the ford tomorrow,” accompanied by the caravan’s route and any known obstruction. This becomes anticipation rather than a fabricated promise. Closing the game freezes the journey.

**Pause rules:** the player’s explicit world pause stops all simulation; the existing tactical pause stops only the watched battle. Closing the game freezes everything. A completed chapter handoff pauses for the next-world decision. Ordinary notifications, screen changes, diary reading, lens switches, celebrations and danger alerts never pause automatically.

**Do/feel:** follow an urgent consequence or return to an anticipated change; maintain curiosity without treating every conversation as an alarm. **Loop:** leave with an expectation, return to what actually happened. **Links:** knowledge, inner lives, live-time pressure.

**E — Replace three spreadsheets with observable behavior**

- **Relationships become offers and responses.** Show who waits at whose door, who leaves a seat open, who accepts help and who turns away. Before a relevant command, a specific statement exposes the condition: “I’ll cross if Mira covers me.” The ribbon joins the people and actions involved. **Do:** arrange the situation or change the plan. **Feel:** recognition of a particular relationship. **Loop:** see whether another shared experience changes that response. **Links:** Bonds, courage, defiance.

- **Knowledge becomes perspective and contradiction.** The piloted lens shows the face, emblem or remembered position this hero recognizes. Another hero can visibly contest that interpretation: their ribbon points elsewhere, accompanied by a concrete objection. Switching lens reveals the difference without briefing either hero. **Do:** get the relevant witness or signal to the person who needs it. **Feel:** discovery. **Loop:** follow the next disagreement. **Links:** testimony, spies, counters.

- **Soul residence becomes a continuous handoff.** Follow the same recognizable presence from vessel to destination. A winter departure leaves the guardian’s threshold dark; its memorial remains. Transit follows an actual carried anchor. Final release disperses the presence while people and traditions continue. **Do:** escort, visit, recover or prepare for absence. **Feel:** continuity and farewell. **Loop:** pursue that person’s unfinished consequence. **Links:** the soul machine, Winter, Last Watch.

Belief already has its own visible progression: private imitation, a publicly recognized emblem, then an actual manifested effect. No conviction meter is needed.

**The cast becomes connected ensembles.** Organize access around real households, squads, schools and institutions. Introduce an emerging person through a recognizable connection **and their own desire**: “Mira’s quiet roommate wants to rebuild the crossing.”

Every resident remains individually reachable, searchable and followable. Anyone can initiate a request or public action; people without prominent friends also get introductions. Following someone keeps their changes foregrounded without imposing a limit on whom the player can follow.

The camera’s attention rotates as stories intersect. A background apprentice can become a foreground founder; an established champion can remain familiar through their school even when seldom deployed. The whole roster continues living.
